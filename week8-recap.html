Money and points
Every team's FAAB spent against its season points. Eight weeks of data, and the line slopes down.
Each dot is a team: dollars spent across the horizontal, points scored up the side. The fitted line falls at about a fifth of a point for every dollar spent, and the correlation is r = −0.35. Before anyone frames that: eleven dots is not evidence of anything, and the causation almost certainly runs backwards — teams spend because they are losing, not the other way round. But it is the fourth straight week the cheapest buy in the room produced the week's high score, and after eight weeks there is still not one number on this page suggesting that a big Wednesday buys a good Sunday. The two dots at the far left are the teams that have barely bid. One of them is now dead, and the other has $200 and first refusal on Bijan Robinson.

Andrew Hunter is the head at 69.70. He went into Sunday leading the league in season points, and he had spent one dollar in eight weeks. Ten remain.

This page is obliged to quote itself, because last Tuesday it wrote: "Andrew Hunter has now taken the best unclaimed thing in two consecutive estate sales without moving his FAAB: Alvin Kamara last week, Tyler Warren this week. He has spent one dollar all season and leads the league in points. There may not be a cleaner argument on this page." The argument lasted six days. Kamara and Warren both go back into the pool tomorrow morning, which is either a joke the format is telling or simply what the format is.

What actually happened is less funny and more ordinary. In Week 5 Skidmark Steve scored 143.88 — the third-best single week anyone has produced this season. Then 98.40, then 96.50, then 69.70. Three consecutive declines, the second-longest run this league has recorded, and the longest one belonged to Travis Elliott, who is also in the basket. He crossed under the league median in Week 6 and never got back above it. A roster can lead the league on total points and still be dying in plain sight, because total points are not what this game counts.

He is the second points leader to go in four weeks. Allen Bradley led by a mile in Week 5 and scored 52.36. Hunter led by 0.38 and scored 69.70. In a head-to-head league both of them are playoff teams; here they are estate sales. Eight heads in and that remains the only durable lesson the paper has: the season total is a vanity statistic and the weekly floor is the whole game.

The money is the other half of it, and this week there is finally enough data to draw it. The chart above plots every team's FAAB spending against its season points. The fitted line slopes down — about a fifth of a point lost per dollar spent — at a correlation of −0.35. That is not a finding and this page will not pretend it is: eleven dots prove nothing, and the obvious reading is that teams spend because they are losing, not that spending makes them lose. But it is worth printing next to this: for the fourth consecutive week, the team that posted the high score had spent almost nothing on Wednesday. $1 and 153.90 in Week 5. $5 and 126.70 in Week 6. $1 and 125.30 in Week 7. Six dollars and 142.90 on Sunday.

That six dollars included Patrick Mahomes for three. The house priced him at $55 last Tuesday and said plainly that the team with the lowest projection and $109 in the bank had a quarterback problem and a sale to fix it with. Ryan Zimmerman instead spent $99 on Saquon Barkley and Chris Olave, which was defensible and produced 98.80. Jimmy Wheeler took Mahomes for three dollars, left him on the bench behind Drake Maye, and scored 142.90. Mahomes may never start a game for him. It was still the best three dollars this league has spent.

At the other end of the sheet, nobody bid on Mark Andrews. A starting tight end, priced here at $30, on a roster that had just scored 58.30 — and not one of eleven teams put in a claim, in a league where half the rosters are starting a tight end they found in free agency. Keenan Allen drew nothing either. Seven of Jordan's fourteen lots went unsold and the room spent $132 on the rest. Meanwhile Brandon Aubrey, who went unclaimed at $20 in last week's sale, went to glenwood for a dollar. The sheet is a suggestion; the room is a weather system.

Eric Allred is now the first team in this league to reach $0. He spent his last $31 on Quinshon Judkins, started him, scored 75.90 and lived by 6.20 — the fourth-closest call anyone alive has survived. He has nine weeks left, no money, and the highest Week 9 projection on the board at 118.4, which is the kind of sentence that only makes sense here. Ryan Kelley, meanwhile, became the first team past 900 points at 902.22 and took over the season lead. Both men who held that spot before him are dead.

Now Skidmark's fifteen go up and it is the richest sheet of the year. Bijan Robinson is in the pool — an every-week starter from the first round of August, and the best running back this sale has ever offered. Garrett Wilson, Jordan Love, Zay Flowers behind him, with Kamara and Tyler Warren making their second and third appearances on these pages in a month. Ten bidders. Ian Zimmerman has $94 and, after dropping Lamar Jackson on Tuesday and Bo Nix last Wednesday, no quarterback on his roster at all — and Jordan Love is on the sheet. And Tony Wetzel, who has not placed a single bid in eight weeks and seven sales, has the only full $200 in the league on the morning Bijan Robinson becomes available. If that doesn't move him, nothing will.

Week 9 opens Thursday on the flattest board of the season — ten teams projected between 87.4 and 118.4, a spread of thirty-one points where last week it was thirty-five. The model likes Gabriel Guglielmo at 30.7 percent and Collin Allison at 23.2, which between them is barely more than a coin flip across two teams out of ten. It has now had eight tries and named the right team once. Read it as weather.
