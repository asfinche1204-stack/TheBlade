The editor
Off with Their Heads!!, 97.70

Exactly the median, which is a new way to be unremarkable. Fourth in season points at 654.46 and the highest Week 7 projection in the league at 117.0, with the smallest wallet at $18 — and four players listed out, one of whom is in the starting lineup as this goes to press. That is the editor's own problem to fix by Sunday.

Travis Elliott's ReekieBoobie is the head at 69.00, and the way he got there is unlike anything else this league has produced. He opened the season with 121.86 — the fourth-highest score of Week 1 — and then scored less every single week after it: 105.0, 102.9, 101.6, 80.30, 69.00. Six weeks, six steps down, no exceptions. Twelve remain.

That streak is the record of the season and nobody is close to it. Across the other seventeen rosters that have played in this league, no team has strung together more than two consecutive declines. Elliott managed five, from the top of the board to the bottom of it, and finishes ninth in total points at 580.66 — ahead of four teams that are still alive. He crossed under the league median in Week 4 and never got back above it.

The quarterback was the visible symptom. This page counted six different starting quarterbacks in six weeks on that roster and said so in print last Tuesday, when 80.30 was the second-worst score of Week 5 and the only reason it survived was that the league's points leader had collapsed on the same afternoon. He went to Bryce Young for Week 6 and scored 69.00. Young, for what it is worth, has 110.46 points on the season and ranks ninth among quarterbacks — he was not the problem. Six quarterbacks in six weeks was.

The model, for the record, had Elliott at 0.7 percent. That is its worst miss of the year by a distance, and the sixth week in a row that the pre-snap favorite was not the team that went. Collin Allison, the favorite at 23.5 percent, scored 94.80 and finished ninth. Six tries, one hit. Read it as weather.

The shave was 10.60. Jordan Vance put Patrick Mahomes back in his lineup and it was worth twenty-two points over the previous week, and 79.60 still left him second from the bottom for a second straight Sunday. Saquon Barkley and Quinshon Judkins are both listed out, so his starting backfield is Pierre Strong Jr. and Jaleel McLaughlin, and he is first in the waiver line tomorrow with the only completely untouched $200 in the league. Six weeks in, Jordan Vance has never placed a bid.

The board itself was the tightest it has been since Week 4 — 57.70 points from Brett Kreiser's 126.70 down to the head, with nine of thirteen teams between 90 and 110. The median came in at 97.70, which happens to be the editor's exact score, and the gold number on the range chart above tells the real story: the spread has gone 97.9, 67.6, 64.9, 55.1, 101.5, 57.7. There is no pattern to find in it. In a narrow week an ordinary 90 is genuinely dangerous; in a wide one a bad 70 walks away clean.

And then there was the sale, which was the biggest this league has run. Five teams spent $304 on dos gatos' estate. Eric Allred paid $131 for Jonathan Taylor, breaking the week-old record by twenty dollars, and now has $31 to his name with Jahmyr Gibbs sitting on his bench. The editor spent $72 and came away with Chuba Hubbard and Rashee Rice, which cost him the wallet and bought him a starter listed out. Collin Allison spent $63 on Brock Purdy and Dalton Kincaid. Ian Zimmerman spent $33 on Nico Collins and DJ Moore, and Moore is out. Brett Kreiser spent $5 on Devaughn Vele and then posted the high score of the week, which is the second time in three weeks that the cheapest buy in the room has been the best one.

Andrew Hunter spent nothing and got Alvin Kamara. His FAAB reads $199 on both sides of the sale. The house put Kamara at $40 and wrote that he was the value in the pool precisely because he had been benched the week his team died — the room would see the bad line and look away. It did. Meanwhile the $111 James Cook III sits on glenwood's bench this week behind Kyren Williams and Jaylen Warren, and Ryan Kelley has $38 to show for the most expensive morning in league history. Five sales now, and the pattern has never once broken: the room overpays at the top, misses the middle in both directions, and leaves real starters in free agency on Wednesday afternoon.

Which brings us to the best lot this paper has ever had to price. Jaxon Smith-Njigba is in the pool — 140.36 points, the number one scoring wide receiver in fantasy football, rostered in 99.9 percent of ESPN leagues. There has not been a player like this available in six weeks and there may not be another. Behind him: Bryce Young, Jalen Coker at 14.0 a week, Rhamondre Stevenson who plays Thursday, Tyler Warren, and Brandon Aubrey — who came out of Joshua Yoakum's estate in the very first sale as part of a one-dollar three-player claim and comes back to the pool as the top-scoring kicker in it. Twelve bidders. Jordan Vance and Tony Wetzel have untouched $200s, Andrew Hunter has $199, and the two teams who most need a receiver have $38 and $18.

Week 7 opens Thursday and the model has a favorite it is unusually confident about. Brett Kreiser, who just posted the week's high score, starts Trevor Lawrence, Bhayshul Tuten, Parker Washington and Cam Little — all four Jacksonville Jaguars, and the Jaguars are on bye. His projection is 56.8, sixteen points below anyone else on the board, and the model makes him 43.7 percent to be the seventh head. He has $126 and tomorrow morning to fix it. Elsewhere: Allen Bradley, the first man in the basket as the points leader, has renamed his dead team 👑 The First Crown 👑, which is either grief or showmanship and in a league like this one barely differs.
