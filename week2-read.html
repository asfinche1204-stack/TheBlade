Andy Camp survived Week 1 by 6.38 because the man beneath him forgot to start a defense. He is not going to survive Week 2 that way. Not Worth Naming is finished at 54.40 — nine starters, all played, all counted — which makes it the lowest score in the league that can't move, and the two teams still beneath it need 1.4 and 0.4 with five players between them still to play tonight and Monday. The house model runs it 300,000 times and Andy goes in 99 of every 100. The paper has been wrong before. It was wrong by 6.38 last week, in Andy's favor. It doesn't expect to be wrong twice.

The math, for the record. Collin Allison's Raleighwood Chargers are at 53.00 with the Chiefs defense tonight against Indianapolis and Jaxson Dart Monday against the Rams, and need 1.4 between them. Andrew Hunter's Angry Team is at 54.00 with Josh Downs tonight and Isaiah Likely and Devin Singletary Monday, and needs 0.4 — one catch, one carry, one extra point's worth of anything. Jordan Vance's team is at 56.00 with Mahomes and Butker still to play and can't be caught, because 56.00 is already more than 54.40. The editor's Off with Their Heads!! is at 56.10 with Stafford and Adams Monday, and the same sentence applies. Fourteen teams are above the line. Two are under it and climbing. One is on it and can't move.

How Andy got here is the part nobody can explain, including the paper. Justin Jefferson, Drake London, Rhamondre Stevenson, Colston Loveland, Jayden Reed, the Chargers defense, and Bo Nix — a lineup that looks like a lineup — produced 54.40 on a full Sunday. Last week the same roster produced 64.34 with Nix scoring 5.44 on a Monday. Two weeks, two bottom-two finishes, and a Week 2 projection of 99.0 that the roster missed by 45. If he goes, Jefferson and London go into the pool Tuesday, and the second estate sale is bigger than the first.

The first one is settled. The editor bought Christian McCaffrey for $107 — the house said $85, expected $95, and the desk that wrote those numbers paid twelve over the high end and then didn't take Kaelon Black, the handcuff it told everyone to take. Ian Zimmerman took Black and Jonathon Brooks together for $12. Collin took Pickens, Odunze, and Engram for $55 across the three and has the first two starting. Joseph Sorrell took Joe Burrow for a share of $60, which looked like a luxury Tuesday and looks like a necessity now that Kyler Murray is out. Jimmy Wheeler got Kelce and the Cowboys defense for $12 combined, which is the bargain of the sale. Travis Elliott got Brandon Aubrey, the best kicker in football, for a dollar at most. Andrew got Downs and Singletary for a dollar and needs both of them tonight and Monday. Four lots went unsold. The league spent $248; $107 of it came from the paper.

Above the line, two things worth printing. Rick started Jayden Daniels, who was out, with Fernando Mendoza on his bench, and finished at 70.10 with a zero in the quarterback slot; he's safe by 15.7 and every one of those points belongs to Andy. The blade doesn't read names. It reads 54.40.
