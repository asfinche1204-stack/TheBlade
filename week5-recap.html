The guillotine took the leader. Allen Bradley's dos gatos campeones went into Sunday with the most points in the league — 492.58 through four weeks, two of the season's five high scores, never once finishing below eleventh — and scored 52.36. It is the lowest number this league has produced, the lowest head of the season, and it belongs to the only team that had never been in danger. Thirteen remain.

The arithmetic of it is almost unkind. 155.9 in Week 1 is still the highest single score of the year. 52.36 is the lowest. The same roster owns both, seven Sundays apart. He was sixty points clear of the league median in September and fifty-two under it in October. And he finishes third in season points among all eighteen teams that ever played — behind only Andrew Hunter and the editor, both of whom are still alive. In a head-to-head league he is a playoff team. Here he is a sale.

What the roster says is simpler than the numbers. Alvin Kamara sat on his bench. Devaughn Vele, a roster-filler all season, started in his FLEX. Travis Etienne was on injured reserve and Tank Bigsby was listed out, which is bad luck, but Kamara on the bench is not. And at 8:04 on Monday night, with the blade already in motion, he dropped DJ Moore for Skyler Bell — a transaction that will show up in the league's activity feed forever, two hours before the roster it belonged to went into the pool. Five heads now, and four of them had points sitting somewhere they couldn't score.

The shave was 5.24, and it is its own story. Jordan Vance scored 57.60 — which would have been the head in three of the four previous weeks — after benching Patrick Mahomes for Michael Penix Jr., a free Sunday-afternoon pickup, and benching Harrison Butker for Chad Ryland. He survived because the points leader of the league had a worse day. He is first in the waiver line tomorrow with a full, untouched $200, and the model makes him 11.7 percent to go next week anyway.

Above all of that, the week was the widest the league has had: 101.54 points from top to bottom. Ryan Zimmerman's Should Have Used Sleeper put up 153.90, the season's high score, three weeks after scoring 62.3 and surviving by 2.8. Skidmark Steve — Andrew Hunter renamed the team on Friday, and the paper prints what ESPN prints — went 143.88 and now leads the season at 579.92. Tony Wetzel and Ian Zimmerman both cleared 133. At the other end, Travis Elliott's ReekieBoobie managed 80.30 with his fifth different starting quarterback in five weeks, and was never closer than 28 points to the basket because of how far dos gatos fell.

Last week's sale is already the most consequential thing that has happened in this league. Ryan Kelley paid $111 for James Cook and $51 for Joe Burrow in the same waiver run — $162 out of $200, a record bid and a record morning — and the house had those two at $80 combined. Brett Kreiser went the other way, taking four lots for $69 and getting Parker Washington for $35 that this desk priced at $3, while Ladd McConkey, priced at $35, went to him for $10. The desk also put Dalton Schultz at $8 and watched the editor claim him for nothing on Wednesday. Three sales in a row now, the same pattern: the room pays a fortune at the top, misprices the middle in both directions, and leaves real starters sitting in free agency on Wednesday morning.

Now dos gatos' fifteen go up, and the headline is Jonathan Taylor. Kamara, Nico Collins, Brock Purdy — the only starting quarterback in the pool — and Chuba Hubbard are behind him, with Etienne as an IR stash for anyone with an open slot. Thirteen bidders. Jordan Vance and Tony Wetzel have untouched $200s; Ryan Kelley has $38 and the editor has $90.

Week 6 opens Thursday. The model likes Collin Allison to go at one in four, on a 68.7 projection that is the lowest on the board — the same Collin Allison who survived Week 2 by 0.4. The blade falls after Monday night. Thirteen necks, and the one it took last week was the best team in the league.
