Allen Bradley's dos gatos campeones put up 155.90, finished before the Sunday night game kicked off, and cleared the field by 32 points — and in this league that earns him exactly what 70 would have: a seat at the table next week. That's the format. Eighteen teams, no matchups, one number a week, and the lowest one goes in the basket. The top of the board is for bragging. The paper lives at the bottom.

Josh Yoakum's Supreme Team is at 49.36 with one player left tonight in Kansas City, and ESPN projects him for 57.1. The number he actually needs is 69.53. Paul Limone's Perfect Team finished Sunday at 69.52, which makes it the lowest score that can't move, and the only way Josh gets off the block is to pass it — 20.2 from a single player — and then have the four teams still sitting between him and Paul keep climbing, which they're projected to do. The house model runs the night 200,000 times. Josh goes in 91 of every 100. You can run it yourself above.

Paul's 69.52 is the line for everybody. If your score is above it you can't be caught, because Paul can't add a point. Twelve teams cleared it Sunday — Jimmy Wheeler's Haste McCracken is the lowest of them at 82.32 — and they can watch Broncos–Chiefs like people. The six who can't: Josh; Andy Camp's Not Worth Naming, 58.90 with one player projected for 16; Jordan Vance at 60.40 with two; Joseph Sorrell at 65.22 with two; Brett Kreiser's Raw Dawgs at 67.40 with two and needing 2.2; and Paul himself, who can only sit there. Andy is the one with a real pulse. One player, and if that player vanishes on the same night Josh's goes off, it's a five-percent kind of bad night.

Above the line the standings are trivia, which is the joke of the whole format. Tony Wetzel's team leads Andrew Hunter's Angry Team 123.80 to 123.38 for second place, and second place pays nothing. Ian Zimmerman named his team Survive and Advance and is 10th at 88.96, doing both. Gabriel Guglielmo named his 1st one out and is 9th at 92.94, not first out. Collin Allison's Raleighwood Chargers sit at 100.10 with a player tonight. The editor's Off with Their Heads!! is 5th at 112.20 with one player left, and ESPN calls it 99 percent safe; the math says 100, since Paul isn't scoring again. This desk will take the point.

Then the part that makes this league different. When the blade drops after Monday night, the losing roster doesn't go home with its owner. It goes into the pool, and everybody still standing bids on it. The first estate sale of the season opens Tuesday, and if it's Josh's roster, the Tuesday edition lists every name on it and what the bidding should look like.
