The escape
Raleigh Raw Dawgs, lived by 31.20

Projected 56.8 with Lawrence, Tuten, Parker Washington and Cam Little all on a bye, and the model's heaviest favorite of the year at 43.7 percent. Seven weeks of this paper saying money doesn't buy points, and here is the week it bought a life.

Jordan Vance is the head at 58.30, and he went in the basket with Patrick Mahomes at quarterback, Saquon Barkley and Quinshon Judkins on his bench, Mark Andrews at tight end, and $175 of his original $200 never spent. Eleven remain.

This page has had him on the front of it for three straight weeks, which is as close to a warning as a newspaper can issue. In Week 5 he scored 57.60 and lived by 5.24, saved only because the league's points leader collapsed the same afternoon. In Week 6 he scored 79.60 and lived by 10.60. No other team in this league has finished inside eleven points of the blade more than once all season; he did it twice in a row, and then he did the third thing. The chart at the top of this page is the whole story in seven bars.

What makes it a story rather than bad luck is the money. Jordan Vance held the only completely untouched $200 in the league through six estate sales. He was first in the waiver line for the last three of them — the position the format hands to whoever is closest to death — and this paper wrote, in print, last Tuesday: "Running backs, urgently. Pierre Strong Jr. and Jaleel McLaughlin are the starting backfield, Barkley and Judkins are both out… First in line with an untouched $200 — the only team that has never bid." On Thursday morning he made the first and only bid of his season: $25 for the Baltimore defense. On Sunday he started Pierre Strong Jr. and Jaleel McLaughlin, scored 58.30, and the Ravens were on the roster for four days.

The counter-example happened in the same week, which is what makes it worth printing. Brett Kreiser went into Wednesday as the model's heaviest favorite of the season — 43.7 percent, a 56.8 projection, sixteen points below anyone else, with Trevor Lawrence, Bhayshul Tuten, Parker Washington and Cam Little all Jaguars and Jacksonville on a bye. He spent $91: $40 on Jalen Coker, $25 on Kyler Murray, $25 on Rhamondre Stevenson, a dollar on the Carolina defense, and Matt Gay for nothing. He benched all four Jaguars. He scored 89.50, finished eleventh of twelve, and was never within thirty points of the blade. That is the first unambiguous case this season of a team buying its way off the block, and it cost two-thirds of his wallet to do it.

It made for the most expensive Wednesday the league has had: $331 changed hands across eight teams, past last week's $304. The headline was Gabriel Guglielmo paying $132 for Jaxon Smith-Njigba, which breaks Jonathan Taylor's seven-day-old record by exactly one dollar and the record before that by twenty-one. This desk had him at $90, which makes six sales in a row the house has been light on the top lot. At some point that stops being an error and starts being a finding: in this league the best available player costs about forty percent more than he looks like he's worth, every single time.

And at the other end of the same morning, Andrew Hunter claimed Tyler Warren — the sixth-highest-scoring tight end in football, priced here at $22 — for nothing at all. That is two consecutive weeks he has taken the best free thing in the sale, after Alvin Kamara last Wednesday. Andrew Hunter has spent one dollar in seven weeks and leads the league in season points at 774.82. Tony Wetzel has spent zero, has churned eleven free agents through his roster, and posted the second-best score of the week at 120.30. Both of them will have $199 and $200 when somebody good dies in November.

Seven of ReekieBoobie's fifteen lots went unclaimed, including Brandon Aubrey — the top-scoring kicker in the pool, priced at $20, who came over in a $1 three-player claim out of the very first estate of the season and nobody wanted him back. Terry McLaurin went unclaimed. Keon Coleman went unclaimed. The pattern this page has described for six straight weeks held again: the room empties its wallet at the top of the sheet and walks past real starters at the bottom of it.

The board itself was ordinary and the result was not. Collin Allison posted 125.30 — the week's high score, off a one-dollar claim for Chris Godwin. That is the third straight week the high score has come from a team that spent five dollars or less on Wednesday, after Ryan Zimmerman's $1 and 153.90 in Week 5 and Brett Kreiser's $5 and 126.70 in Week 6. Tony Wetzel went 120.30, Jimmy Wheeler 111.30 with Justin Jefferson finally healthy, Ian Zimmerman 111.20. The median was 103.30, the top-to-bottom gap was 67.0, and the editor finished eighth at 99.40 — fifth in season points, seventh in Week 8's projections, and still carrying the smallest wallet in the league at $18 with six men on the injury report.

Now Jordan's fourteen go up and it is, on paper, the best estate of the year after dos gatos'. Patrick Mahomes is available, and the team projected lowest for Week 8 is starting Bryce Young with Josh Allen on his bench and has $109 to fix it. Saquon Barkley is available, listed out, which by this league's own revealed behavior means he will go cheap to someone patient. Judkins, Mark Andrews, Chris Olave and Keenan Allen are behind them. Eleven bidders, and two of them have $199 and $200.

Week 8 opens Thursday. The model likes Ryan Zimmerman at 36.6 percent on a 73.9 projection that is nearly fourteen points below anyone else — the same Ryan Zimmerman who owns the season's high score and the second-closest call still standing. The model has now had seven tries and named the right team once, and last week it named a man who answered by spending $91 and living by thirty-one. Read it as weather.
