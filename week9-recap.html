The playbook
Four free claims, one funeral

Ryan Zimmerman took Alvin Kamara, Zay Flowers, Jordan Addison and Isaiah Likely out of Skidmark's estate for nothing at all — the exact move this page praised in Week 7 and buried in Week 8. Two weeks, two managers, same method, same result. The free lot is free for a reason the room keeps relearning.

The eleven-dollar week
Jordan Love, $11

Ian Zimmerman had no quarterback at all on Tuesday morning. He paid eleven dollars for one, started him, and posted 152.60 — the second-best single week of the entire season. He is also the model's favorite to be the next head at 32.2 percent, because his Week 10 projection is 78.2. Both of those things are true.

Ryan Zimmerman is the head at 63.80, and the number that matters is not that one. It is 153.90 — the score he posted in Week 5, the second-highest single week anyone has managed all season. Nine teams remain, which is half the league.

The chart near the top of this page is the one to look at. It plots every team's worst week against its best, and Should Have Used Sleeper's bar is the longest one still standing when Sunday began: 62.30 at the bottom, 153.90 at the top, 91.6 points of daylight between them. Only one team has ever been wilder, and that was Allen Bradley, who went out in Week 5 holding the league's biggest lead. Three of the four widest ranges in this competition now belong to teams in the basket.

At the other end of the same chart are the two narrowest ranges in the league — 25.4 and 25.8 — and both belong to teams that are still here. This format does not pay for ceilings. It bills you for floors, every single Sunday, and it does not care that you once scored 153.90. Ryan Zimmerman finishes tenth in season points among the eighteen teams that started this thing, and he is gone. Gabriel Guglielmo, who has never cracked 115 in nine weeks, finishes ninth — one place above him — and is still here. That is the whole format in two sentences.

What he did on Wednesday is worth printing too, because this page has now written the same obituary twice. He claimed Alvin Kamara, Zay Flowers, Jordan Addison and Isaiah Likely out of Skidmark Steve's estate for nothing — four free lots, no FAAB moved. That is precisely the method this desk called "the cleanest argument on this page" in Week 7, watched kill Andrew Hunter in Week 8, and has now watched kill a second manager in Week 9. The free lot is free because the room has looked at it and declined; sometimes the room is wrong, and most of the time it simply is not.

And then the opposite happened in the same sale, which is why this one is fun. Tony Wetzel finally bid. Eight weeks, seven estate sales, a roster churned through free agency the whole way, and not a single dollar — and then he put $150 on Bijan Robinson, eighteen past the record Gabriel Guglielmo set for Jaxon Smith-Njigba, and three-quarters of the only full budget left in the league. He scored 139.20, passed 1000 points, and took the season lead. The house had Bijan at $95 and said exactly one team could pay what he was worth. It was right about who, and $55 short on how much.

Below him, Ian Zimmerman paid eleven dollars for Jordan Love — a quarterback for a roster that had none at all, after dropping Lamar Jackson on Tuesday and Bo Nix the week before — and promptly scored 152.60, the second-best week of the year. Brett Kreiser paid five dollars for Tyler Warren and went from 106.00 to 142.80, the biggest week-on-week gain of anyone who bought anything. Which makes it five straight weeks that the high score has come from a team that spent eleven dollars or less on the Wednesday before. The updated money chart on this page has the correlation at −0.37, almost exactly where it sat last Tuesday, and the same warnings apply: ten dots, and the causation almost certainly runs backwards.

The editor finished ninth of ten at 97.80 and was never in it — 34.00 clear, the widest shave of the season — which is less a boast than the only thing this roster does. Off with Their Heads!! has a range of 25.4 points across nine weeks, the narrowest in the league, and a floor of 93.2, set in Week 4 and never broken since. It has also never scored 120. In a head-to-head league that is a .500 team; here it is the shape you want. The tight end slot currently reads Empty with Dallas Goedert on the bench, which is the precise mistake that made Joshua Yoakum the first head of the season, and which will be corrected well before Sunday. The paper notes it because it would note it about anyone else.

Now the sale, and it is cruel. Ryan Zimmerman's fifteen lots are the richest sheet this desk has ever written: Josh Allen, Saquon Barkley, Malik Nabers, Trey McBride, with Ladd McConkey, Chris Olave and Zay Flowers behind them, priced at $442 all told. Nine bidders. They have $338 between them. The biggest single wallet in the league is Ian Zimmerman's $83, Eric Allred has nothing at all, and the man who could have bought this entire estate outright a week ago spent $150 on a running back on Wednesday. Josh Allen is going to go for about a third of what he is worth and somebody is going to look very clever in December.

Alvin Kamara, for the record, enters this pool for the third time. Skidmark Steve claimed him free in Week 6 and is dead; Should Have Used Sleeper claimed him free nine days ago and is dead. He has been a free lot twice and both men who took him are in the basket, which is either the funniest coincidence on these pages or a warning somebody should finally read. Saquon Barkley is back for the second time in three weeks, having cost $59 out of Jordan Vance's estate and lasted one Sunday with his new owner.

Week 10 opens Thursday and the model has a shape it has drawn before: Ian Zimmerman at 32.2 percent on a 78.2 projection, seven days after posting the second-best score of the season. It made exactly this call about Brett Kreiser in Week 7, when he was 43.7 percent, and he spent $91 on Wednesday and survived by thirty-one. Gabriel Guglielmo is right behind at 28.8 on 79.8. At the safe end, Ryan Kelley is projected 125.8 and comes in at 0.3 percent — the lowest number on this week's board by a factor of seven. The model has had nine tries and named the right team once. Read it as weather.

The house vs the room
What this desk priced Skidmark's lots at, and what the league actually paid.

The room now pays almost everything for the top lot and almost nothing for the next nine. Five lots drew no bid at all.
