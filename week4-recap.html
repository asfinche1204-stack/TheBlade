In Week 4 it finished last of fifteen. The league is scoring more every week, and the floor is coming up underneath everybody.

Joseph Sorrell did not go quietly. In the six days before the blade fell he made four roster moves: Kyler Murray out for Bryce Young, Bryce Young out for Austin Ekeler, the Broncos defense out for the Commanders, and Joe Mixon added off the wire on Sunday morning. That is a man reading the same paper everybody else is. It bought him 79.5, an 18-point miss on his projection, and a basket. His four weeks read 77.12, 93.0, 107.6, 79.5 — he was climbing until he wasn't.

The shave was 5.1. Ian Zimmerman's Survive and Advance scored 84.6 after missing its projection by 21.6, the second-worst miss on the board, and lived. Ryan Zimmerman's Sleeper was next at 87.5, which means the Zimmermans occupied two of the bottom three for the second time in three weeks. Gabriel Guglielmo's 1st one out was 12th at 91.7 with a $44 quarterback, and the editor was 11th at 93.2, which is the closest Off with Their Heads!! has come to anything.

Up top it was the best week the league has had. Eric Allred's Rick put up 134.6 — 33.0 over projection, high score of the season's highest-scoring week — and his line now reads 104.46, 70.1, 104.8, 134.6, with the turn arriving the same week Tyler Shough did. Brett Kreiser's Raw Dawgs were second at 129.6 and have still never been within 42 points of a blade. Andrew Hunter's Angry Team was third at 125.0 and carries the league's highest Week 5 projection; nine days ago he was under the line needing 0.4. Allen Bradley's dos gatos campeones stopped the slide at 124.3 and leads the season at 492.58. Nine of fifteen teams beat their projection. The median was 107.3. That is why 79.5 was fatal.

Paul's estate sold well and the receipts are already strange. Jimmy Wheeler paid $55 for Justin Jefferson — $18 less than Paul paid for him six days earlier — and Jefferson is now on Haste's injured reserve, listed out. Gabriel took Jalen Hurts for $44 and finished 12th anyway. Ryan Zimmerman spent $50 on TreVeyon Henderson and Malik Nabers and finished 13th anyway. Andrew Hunter waited a day and took Brian Thomas Jr. for nothing. Ryan Kelley rebuilt eight roster spots on Thursday without spending a dollar and dropped 15 points. The house called Henderson $7 light and Golden $8 light, and was $25 high on Brian Thomas Jr., who nobody bid on at all. Three sales in, the pattern holds: the room overpays for the headline and ignores the third-best lot.

Which brings up the only question that matters today. Joseph's fourteen are up, and they are better than Andy's: James Cook III, Rashee Rice, Ladd McConkey, Joe Burrow, Tucker Kraft, and Joe Mixon, who was a free pickup on Sunday and is now worth a real bid. Fourteen bidders, eleven of them with $138 or more. The editor has $90 and the second-smallest wallet belongs to Jimmy Wheeler at $108, who just spent $75 on a receiver who's on IR.

Week 5 opens Thursday. The model likes Jordan Vance to go, at one in three, on a projection of 68.3 that is nine points below anyone else's — but it liked Joseph in Week 1 and he survived three more weeks, so read it as weather. The blade falls after Monday night. Fourteen necks.

Bidding in a guillotine
What four sales have taught this desk
  • Wait one day. Brian Thomas Jr. was a $25 lot on Tuesday and a free pickup on Wednesday. Every sale so far has leaked a real starter into free agency after waivers cleared.
  • The floor is rising. 79.5 was safe in September and fatal in October; a roster built to score 85 is now a roster on the block. Budget against next month's median, not last month's.
  • Moves aren't points. Nine free transactions in a week moved two teams down and nobody up. Buy the one player who starts, then leave the roster alone.
