The editor
Off with Their Heads!!, 113.9

Third, and the most consistent roster in the league: 115.5, 118.6, 113.9, never lower than 54.4 above a head. Darren Waller started at tight end over a Goedert who was listed out — the paper took its own advice for once. It also has $93, the smallest budget in the league by $37.

Paul Limone is the third name on the wall, and of the three he is the one the paper warned. Two weeks ago this page called Paul's Perfect Team a tightrope: bottom three both weeks, the lowest two-week total in the league by 33.4, a full $200 in the budget, second in the waiver line, and if there was ever a week to spend, that was it. He spent. He put $73 on Justin Jefferson last Wednesday — the biggest bid either sale has seen, $3 over the house price — added the Steelers defense for a dollar on Friday, was projected for 108.0 on Sunday morning, and scored 59.5. Three weeks, 192.02 points, and a roster that is now fourteen lots.

The margin was 2.8. Ryan Zimmerman's Should Have Used Sleeper scored 62.3 with Josh Allen at quarterback and missed its own projection by 34.7, which in any other week is the story. Jimmy Wheeler's Haste McCracken was next at 70.2. Those two, plus Paul, were the only teams under 84 — and Paul was the only one under 62. In Week 2 the shave was 0.4. In Week 3 it was 2.8. The format is tightening a week at a time.

The roster tells the rest. When the blade fell, De'Von Achane — on injured reserve — was sitting in one of Paul's starting running back slots. That's three heads and three dead starting slots: Josh with nobody at all at D/ST in Week 1, Andy with 46.96 points on his bench in Week 2, Paul with a man on IR in his lineup in Week 3. Nobody in this league has yet been eliminated while fielding their best nine. The blade has not had to be sharp.

Above him, the week belonged to Ryan Kelley. glenwood gill-o-teen went 94.16, 107.1, 124.4 — up every week — and took the high score off Allen Bradley, whose dos gatos campeones has gone 155.9, 122.0, 90.4 in the other direction and is out of the top ten for the first time. Andrew Hunter was under the line and needing 0.4 nine days ago; he scored 116.3 and finished second. Ian Zimmerman's Survive and Advance turned $44 of Andy's estate into 107.9 after a 78.0. Eric Allred's Rick paid $38 for Tyler Shough, has scored 104.8, and looks like the best buy of the season so far. The editor's Off with Their Heads!! was third at 113.9 and remains the only team never to finish within 54 of a head.

Which raises the only question this paper cares about on a Tuesday: does buying work? Seven teams spent something out of the second sale. Five of them scored more in Week 3 than in Week 2, three by more than 29 points. The two who didn't were Jimmy Wheeler, who spent $5, and Paul, who spent $74 and is the reason there's a sale at all. Money doesn't save a lineup. It just gives the lineup better players to leave in the wrong slot.

Paul's fourteen are the best pool of the season and it isn't close: Jalen Hurts, Justin Jefferson, Malik Nabers, Brian Thomas Jr., TreVeyon Henderson, and De'Von Achane on IR for anyone with an open slot to stash him. Jefferson goes back up seven days after selling for $73, which is either the bargain of the year or a lesson, depending on who wins him. Fifteen bidders, and thirteen of them still have $130 or more. The editor has $93 and will be outbid on everything that matters.

Week 4 opens Thursday night. The next blade falls after Monday. Fifteen necks, and the three that have already gone all had the points somewhere on the roster.

Bidding in a guillotine
What three sales have taught this desk
  • The room pays up at the top and nothing at the bottom. Jefferson and Shough went for house price or better; Tucker, Stevenson, and Nix went for zero. If you want a mid-tier lot, wait for the free-agent window instead of bidding.
  • Buying works — five of seven buyers scored more the following week, three of them by 29 points or more — but it only works if the player starts. Paul bought the best lot in the sale and still went out.
  • Watch the budgets, not just the board. Raleigh Raw Dawgs has $200, no injuries, and no hole. That's the team that takes Hurts or Jefferson if it wants them.
